01 · Essence

A burger that is also the menu

The hamburger menu is three lines that hide a pile of options. Whoprrr is a pile of Mac utilities that stays out of sight until needed, so the mark is a burger: it is the menu button, and it is the app.

The name

Say “whop-rrr”. The rrr is the sound of a machine doing something for you: a purr, a rev. Write it Whoprrr: capital W, the rest lowercase, one word.

The idea

Layers you can stack, drop or swap. One bun is the chrome, the middle is signal: display signal above, audio waveform below. It looks like what the app does.

The personality

Playful on the outside, exact inside. The logo can be silly; the behaviour is not. When it works it is quiet. When it fails it says so plainly.

Tagline

Your Mac, stacked your way. Short forms: “Stack it your way.” · “One menu bar. Everything else, off.”

02 · Logo

The burger

  1. Top bun. The menu. Always bun yellow with sesame.
  2. Subpixel row. Twelve chunky columns, red, green, blue, repeating: the display and video side (Samsung control, window layout).
  3. Waveform patty. Fifteen mirrored bars in pink, loudest in the middle: the audio side. It is a patty, so it stays low and wide.
  4. Bottom bun. A flat base with rounded heels.
  5. Plate. Violet gradient, 22.5% corner radius. The burger fills 84% of its width.

Forms

App icon

Sesame drops out below 40 px. Never smaller than 16 px.

Mark only

For dark or violet grounds when the plate would double up. Keep clear space of one column width on all sides.

Wordmark

Whoprrr

SF Pro Rounded Heavy, tracking −3%. The rrr is bright violet with a red and blue split shadow (0.07 em). Use the split on large sizes only; flat violet under 20 pt.

Don’t

Stretched
Recoloured RGB
Outlined
Rotated

Also: no other bun colour, no extra layers (cheese, lettuce, bacon) in the official mark, no drop shadow baked into the artwork, and never use the name or mark next to anyone else’s burger trademark.

03 · Menu-bar glyph

One colour, 18 points

The menu bar only accepts a template image, so the glyph is a single colour and macOS tints it for light and dark. It keeps the three signals of the full mark: dome bun, six dashes for the subpixel row, nine mirrored bars for the waveform, flat base.

18 pt dark

Source: MenuBarIcon.swift (24-unit grid drawn at 0.75). Don’t colour it, don’t add a badge dot (use the panel’s status chip), keep the height at 18 pt.

06 · Interface

Two surfaces, two jobs

The menu-bar panel is for glancing and doing things right now: status and the few toggles you reach for during the day. The main window is the app: a sidebar of features and a roomy page for each, where everything is set up. The panel has a Settings button (⌘,) and nothing else that needs configuring.

Menu-bar panel

400 pt wide. Header (logo, wordmark, settings gear), a permission banner only when something needs fixing, one card per installed feature with its status and one control, then Settings… and Quit. Window buttons act on the app you were in, not on Whoprrr.

Main window

Sidebar 232 pt (logo, features, Features, Permissions, status chip) and a page up to 680 pt wide, minimum window 860 × 620. Opens on Permissions the first time. While it is open Whoprrr also shows in the Dock; closing it returns to menu-bar only.

07 · Voice

Warm, short, honest

Talk like a helpful friend who knows the Mac: plain words, one idea per sentence, no exclamation marks, no puns in places where something is wrong. The jokes live in the logo and the name; the copy gets on with it.

Say
  • “Needs Input Monitoring to see keys.” (what, then the fix)
  • “Imported 6 rules; 1 skipped.”
  • “Quit Karabiner-Elements so the same rule does not run twice.”
  • “Paused. Press ⌃⌥⇧⌘⎋ to resume.”
Don’t say
  • “Oops! Something went wrong 🍔”
  • “Whoprrr couldn’t be bothered.”
  • “Error 1190: invalid state.”
  • “Please grant the necessary permissions to continue.”

Words we use

Rule (not mapping), Keys and mouse, Mic (the processed one is Whoprrr Mic), Features (installed or not), Permissions. Say “press” for keys, “click” for buttons, “hold” for dual-role keys. Name shortcuts with symbols (⌃⌥⇧⌘) and name keys as they are printed.

Regenerate

swift build -c release && WHOPPER_RENDER_ICON=docs/brand/assets .build/release/Whopper writes the iconset, AppIcon.icns and the PNGs from the SwiftUI artwork, so the shipped icon and the in-app logo are the same drawing. python3 scripts/brand/gen_manual.py rewrites this manual, the SVGs and the tokens.
